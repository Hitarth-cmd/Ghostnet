# Sea Turtles and Marine Debris (DEMO knowledge-base document)

Sea turtles are particularly vulnerable to marine debris for two main
reasons: entanglement in derelict fishing gear, which can restrict
movement, cause injury, or lead to drowning; and ingestion of debris,
especially floating plastic that can resemble jellyfish (a common prey
item), which can cause internal injury or blockages.

Nesting beaches and nearshore foraging habitat are especially sensitive
because debris accumulating there can affect both adult turtles and
hatchlings. Monitoring priorities near turtle habitat typically weigh
gear type (netting poses higher entanglement risk than rigid debris),
proximity to nesting season, and proximity to the habitat boundary.
